Popularity vs. Being Load-Bearing

Degree measures how many connections a character has. Betweenness measures how many shortest paths depend on them. On the Marvel character network's giant component, plotting one against the other on a log–log scale — then asking who over-performs what their own degree predicts — surfaces a character with only two connections who turns out to be structurally irreplaceable, and a handful of other genuine bridges that raw popularity alone would never reveal.

The Rockman Surprise

A log-log scatter of betweenness centrality against degree, fit with a trend line, surfaces which nodes over- or under-perform what their own degree would predict.

Discussion question

“Who is the most surprising character in the network by any centrality, against the right null? Make the case, with a figure, and tell us what they bridge.”

Answer

Rockman_(character). Degree 2 — connected only to Black_Widow_(Natasha_Romanova) and The_Witness_(character) — yet the single largest positive residual in the entire betweenness-vs-degree scatter below: +2.02, far above what any other degree-2 node reaches.

The reason: The_Witness_(character) is a degree-1 dead end, and Rockman is its only link to the rest of the network. Every shortest path from The_Witness_(character) to anyone else has to pass through him — so low degree doesn't mean low importance, if you happen to be someone's sole bridge to everywhere else.

What surprised me: betweenness centrality alone can actually overstate a node's importance. Rockman's whole score comes from one fact — Witness has no other way out — not from bridging anything large or structurally significant. Look at his actual impact on the network instead of his betweenness value: removing him leaves a giant component of 275 nodes, exactly tied with Deadpool, Deathlok, Genis-Vell, Guardsman_(character), Moon_Knight, and Nightmask — completely unremarkable, nowhere near Spider-Man (271, and it fragments into 6 pieces instead of 2). What's genuinely unusual about Rockman isn't his importance to the network — it's his efficiency: no other node gets this much betweenness from only 2 connections. The real claim this page is making is narrower, and more honest, than "most important character": Rockman is the most surprising character relative to what his own degree predicts, not the most structurally critical one.

Try It on the Real Network

The full 277-node giant component renders as an unreadable hairball, so pick a smaller, self-contained neighborhood to browse instead — computed on the fly with the same breadth-first search from earlier, just bounded to a fixed number of hops from one (or several) characters. Click any node to remove it: node size is degree (how many connections, right now — it shrinks live as you cut a node's neighbors), and node color is betweenness centrality on a sequential scale from pale lilac to deep purple — a from-scratch Brandes' algorithm running in your browser, recomputed and cached once per click rather than continuously. Faded, translucent nodes have been stranded outside the current biggest cluster.

Each view below combines two or three bridge characters' neighborhoods into one: a single character's 1-hop neighborhood alone is basically a star, where every shortest path already runs through the center and betweenness has nowhere to redistribute onto as you remove nodes. These combined views avoid that — each pair's neighborhoods overlap only at a couple of shared hub characters, so removing one bridge forces its leftover neighbors to route through whatever connection is left. Watch for a small, pale node that suddenly darkens once its neighborhood's other exit disappears.

Each pairing puts two of the bridge characters from earlier in the same view: Hercules (cosmic/Avengers-adjacent teammates), Black Widow (the spy/espionage cluster), and U.S. Agent (a cluster of lower-profile military/team-support characters) each anchor their own corner, joined to the others only through a couple of shared hub characters — Spider-Man and Wolverine show up again and again. Try Hercules + U.S. Agent and click Hercules first: Ares_(Marvel_Comics) — still just degree 3 — jumps roughly 4.7× in betweenness as soon as Hercules is gone, becoming a brand-new bridge in a network he wasn't previously load-bearing in at all. All three bridges together gives the richest version of this: three separate neighborhoods, several shared hub connectors, and the most room for betweenness to genuinely move around as you remove nodes. Rockman_(character) himself doesn't appear in any of these views — his effect depends on a degree-1 dead end (The_Witness_(character)) that none of these neighborhoods happen to include; see the figure above and the isolated toy demo below for that mechanism specifically.

Try it: the Rockman effect, isolated

A small synthetic graph: a dense 5-node hub clique, one bridge node with degree exactly 2, and a variable number of leaves hanging off the bridge. Betweenness recomputed live (Brandes' algorithm) as you drag the slider.

Leaves hanging off the bridge 6
Drag "leaves" up: the bridge's degree never changes — it always has exactly 2 neighbors, the hub side and one leaf-facing side — but its betweenness climbs because more nodes depend on it as their only route. Now tick the "back door" box: once leaves have an alternative path to the hub, the bridge's betweenness collapses toward zero even though nothing about its own degree changed. That's the Rockman effect, isolated.
