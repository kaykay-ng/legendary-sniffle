🕷️ Marvel Network Analysis

Understanding Degree Distributions: From Linear to Logarithmic Scale

📊 Dataset Overview

This analysis examines the Wikipedia Marvel Comics superhero network:

303 Characters
All superheroes in Wikipedia's Marvel Comics category
1,784 Directed Edges
Links between characters in Wikipedia articles

We focus on in-degree: how many characters link TO each character (popularity/importance).

Step 1️⃣ Linear Scale

Starting with the familiar linear scale (the way most people visualize data). The y-axis shows raw frequency, the x-axis shows in-degree.

What we see: The distribution appears to have a steep initial drop, with most characters having low in-degree. The visualization is dominated by the first few bars, making it hard to see patterns at higher degrees.

Step 2️⃣ Normalized Linear Scale

Converting to proportions (fraction of total population) instead of raw counts. This makes it easier to interpret as percentages.

What we see: Same shape, but now we can see that ~15-20% of characters have in-degree 0, ~10% have in-degree 1, etc. The high-degree tail is still compressed visually.

Step 3️⃣ Logarithmic Scale (Unbinned)

Switching to a log-log scale - both axes are logarithmic. This is where power laws reveal their true nature.

What we see: The plot looks jagged and noisy! This is because at high degrees, there are very few nodes (frequency = 1 or 2). Each degree value creates a separate point, revealing the discrete nature of the data. The noise obscures the underlying pattern.

Key insight: The jaggedness is NOT an error - it's showing the real sparsity at high degrees. But it makes the trend hard to see.

Step 4️⃣ Logarithmic Scale (Binned)

Aggregating nearby degrees into bins to smooth out the noise and reveal the true pattern.

🔴 MAJOR FINDING: The distribution is NOT a simple power law! There's a steep drop between degrees 50-100 (highlighted in red) that's much sharper than the overall trend suggests. This indicates a "broken power law" with an exponential cutoff.

💡 What Does This Mean?

Power Law Exponent: -0.81

The overall fit suggests a gentle power law, but this masks the complex behavior.

The Distribution Has Two Regimes:

  • Low-to-Mid Degrees (1-40): Gentle power law falloff. Most Marvel characters have moderate linking.
  • High Degrees (50-100+): Sharp drop with exponential cutoff. Very few characters have extremely high in-degrees.

Why the steep drop?

  • Wikipedia article length limits how many characters can be mentioned
  • Natural ceiling on how "connected" a character can be in the network
  • Common pattern in real-world networks with physical/resource constraints

📈 Why Scale Matters

Scale What It Shows Best For
Linear Dominated by low values; high values compressed General audience; intuitive view
Log-Log Power laws appear as straight lines; reveals all scales equally Network analysis; detecting power laws
Binned Log-Log Smooths noise; reveals breaks and regime changes Identifying multiple scaling regimes

🎯 Conclusion

The journey from linear to logarithmic scale reveals a hidden story in the Marvel network:

The degree distribution follows a power law with exponential cutoff - not a simple power law. There's a gentle gradual decrease for low-to-mid degrees, then a sharp cutoff at high degrees. This pattern is characteristic of networks with finite resources (like Wikipedia article lengths) that naturally limit extreme hubs.

Lesson: Always visualize network data on multiple scales! A single perspective can hide important structure in the data.
